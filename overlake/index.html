The latest in Overlake

November 12, 2026

Overlake Square, 15117 and 15171 NE 24th Street, would replace the existing commercial buildings with an eight-story mixed-use building. The city's page for file LAND-2023-00031 now carries a notice of decision. City of Redmond

November 5, 2026

The city's Overlake page lists dozens of projects, among them the NE 51st Street improvements, the 156th Avenue cycle track and the Overlake Village Station infiltration vault. None shows a new dated milestone this week. City of Redmond

October 29, 2026

152nd Avenue NE, from NE 20th Street to the Overlake Village light rail station, is being remade as Overlake Village's main street. The city's share, built from October 2022 to October 2024, is done; private development fills in the rest through 2030, toward one travel lane each way, a center turn lane, protected bike lanes, street parking, planting areas and wide sidewalks. City of Redmond

October 22, 2026

Esterra Park, 2808 Calder Avenue NE, is 2.67 acres of privately owned public space: a plaza, an amphitheater, tree groves, a meadow of native wildflowers, public art, an accessible elevator and a hill climb to the light rail station. It opened in November 2023. City of Redmond

October 15, 2026

The Overlake Village Pedestrian Bridge carries walkers and riders from Overlake Village Station across SR 520 to the 520 Bike Trail. Look up on the way over: "Verdant," by Seattle artist Leo Saul Berk, is 1,026 vertical louvers with more than 156,000 hand-painted marks that form a pixelated Northwest forest. City of Redmond

October 12, 2026

Wednesday's Planning Commission hearing also adds a shared-use path on the north side of NE 40th Street, between 158th and 156th Avenues NE, to the city's transportation project list. During the October 17–18 shuttle weekend, Overlake Village and Redmond Technology stations keep 10-minute trains. Planning Commission memo

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Redmond newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Redmond that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Redmond's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It goes to a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher only if you tick the sharing box on the form; that brokerage may then contact you about your home. Leave the box empty and it stays with us. Never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
