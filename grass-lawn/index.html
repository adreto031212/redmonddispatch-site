The latest in Grass Lawn

November 12, 2026

Curb ramps are being rebuilt to current accessibility standards on all four corners along 140th Avenue NE, from NE 80th Street to NE 69th Place, through November, weekdays from 7 AM to 5:30 PM. It is one stretch of a 47-ramp project across Grass Lawn and Education Hill that the city expects to finish in December. City of Redmond

November 5, 2026

The city lists its Grass Lawn Park parking lot project, off 148th Avenue NE, as complete. The lot was repaved, with new curb ramps, sidewalks, stormwater pipe and catch basins, and 22 trees were replaced. City of Redmond

October 29, 2026

Evenings come early after Sunday's time change, and Grass Lawn Park's six courts are lit; from December through February the lights stay on until 10 PM. The park, 7031 148th Avenue NE, also has a basketball court, a playground, multi-use turf and walking paths. City of Redmond

October 22, 2026

On a hillside on Redmond's west side, Grass Lawn looks over the Sammamish Valley to the Cascades in the north and east. It is mostly homes, with two small commercial zones, and Grass Lawn Park is its anchor. City of Redmond

October 15, 2026

The Grass Lawn Park parking lot on 148th Avenue NE is rebuilt: new pavement, curb ramps and sidewalks, new storm drains, and 22 trees replaced. The old sycamores and plane trees had pushed roots into the pipes and pavement; Redpoint maples, hornbeams and magnolias now grow in structural soil. City of Redmond

October 12, 2026

Grass Lawn Park, 7031 148th Avenue NE, has six lit tennis courts, a pavilion, a picnic shelter and a summer splash pad. Softball Field 1 was resurfaced and now carries Redmond's first cricket practice pitch. Park page · Field project

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Redmond newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Redmond that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Redmond's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It goes to a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher only if you tick the sharing box on the form; that brokerage may then contact you about your home. Leave the box empty and it stays with us. Never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
