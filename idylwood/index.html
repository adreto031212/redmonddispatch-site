The latest in Idylwood

November 12, 2026

The city has approved replacing a damaged pedestrian bridge over Brae Burn Creek on the Brae Burn golf course, 2409 182nd Avenue NE. The file is SEPA-2024-00149, with a determination of non-significance on record. City of Redmond

November 5, 2026

The city is in design on a renovation of the main parking lot at Idylwood Park, with new ADA-compliant stalls, paving and a drainage system. Construction is scheduled to start in early 2027 and finish by early summer 2027. City of Redmond

October 29, 2026

A shared-use path for people walking and biking is planned along the south side of NE 40th Street, from 163rd to 172nd Avenue NE, for better access to Redmond Technology Station. It is in design, with construction expected to start in spring 2027; the design may become a street-level path with a buffer in place of the two-way left-turn lane. City of Redmond

October 22, 2026

The main parking lot at Idylwood Park is next: the city will expand and renovate it to fix failed pavement, drainage, damaged curbs and accessibility gaps. It is in design now; construction is scheduled to start in early 2027 and finish by early summer 2027. City of Redmond

October 15, 2026

Idylwood Park, 3650 West Lake Sammamish Parkway NE, is the only free recreational access on Lake Sammamish. Car-top boats launch from the small ramp, anglers fish from the pier or the beach, and the beach has no lifeguards. City of Redmond

October 12, 2026

A new water main is going in along NE 24th Street, working west from West Lake Sammamish Parkway NE through November, Monday to Thursday, 7 AM to 5 PM. Expect lane shifts or flaggers with one lane alternating. Final repaving is planned for 2027. City of Redmond

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Redmond newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Redmond that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Redmond's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It goes to a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher only if you tick the sharing box on the form; that brokerage may then contact you about your home. Leave the box empty and it stays with us. Never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
