The latest in Southeast Redmond

November 12, 2026

A charging and maintenance facility for a 291-vehicle electric commute and shuttle fleet is proposed at 19150 Union Hill Road. The files are LAND-2023-00271 and SEPA-2024-00001, under review, with a notice of application posted. City of Redmond

November 5, 2026

A proposed temple at 17602 Union Hill Road would change an existing office building to a temple use, with no exterior changes to the site. The city's file is LAND-2026-00111. The application is under review, and the page lists no comment deadline. City of Redmond

October 29, 2026

A shared-use path is going in along NE 70th Street to Redmond Way, with a new crosswalk on the north leg of the Redmond Way and NE 70th Street intersection and a pedestrian island at its northwest corner, for better walking and biking links to the Marymoor Village light rail station. Construction began this summer and is expected to finish by spring 2027. City of Redmond

October 22, 2026

Southeast Redmond Park, 3.2 acres at 188th Avenue NE and NE 67th Street, is in early design. Council approved the design contract on March 3, 2026; the park will include walking and biking paths and serve as a buffer between the industrial and residential areas, and construction is planned for late 2027. City of Redmond

October 15, 2026

A change on Wednesday's Planning Commission agenda adds a shared-use path to the planned new NE 72nd Street east of 188th Avenue NE, alongside curb, sidewalk, lighting and storm drainage; the project is estimated at $2.1 million and listed as near term. Planning Commission memo

October 12, 2026

Tuesday's committee takes up declaring the former Pacer Propane lot at 18459 NE 76th Street surplus so it can be sold; the city bought it as temporary space while rebuilding its maintenance center, which is moving to a new site. Wednesday's hearing adds a planned shared-use path on NE 70th Street from 188th Avenue NE to the Evans Creek Trail. Committee memo · Planning Commission memo

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Redmond newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Redmond that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Redmond's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It goes to a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher only if you tick the sharing box on the form; that brokerage may then contact you about your home. Leave the box empty and it stays with us. Never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
