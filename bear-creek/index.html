The latest in Bear Creek

November 5, 2026

The city's Bear Creek page lists eight projects, among them the Avondale Road pavement work from NE 90th Street to Novelty Hill Road and the Evans Creek relocation. None shows a new dated milestone this week. City of Redmond

October 29, 2026

On the east side of Avondale Road's 10000 block, north of Novelty Hill Road, Bear Creek had slowly worked its way into the road embankment, settling it and undercutting the sidewalk. The city re-channeled that stretch of creek, added large woody debris and a backwater side channel, replanted the bank with native plants and stabilized the embankment; construction is complete. City of Redmond

October 22, 2026

Where Bear Creek and Evans Creek meet, the city's Keller Farm Wetland Mitigation Bank covers 83 acres at 8810 Avondale Road NE. Redmond bought the farm in 2015; the state approved the bank in December 2019, grading finished in 2020, and log jams in Bear Creek were added for salmon habitat along with three acres of stream-side planting. City of Redmond

October 15, 2026

Evans Creek is being moved away from the industrial area north of NE Union Hill Road to give salmon better habitat, with 12 acres of native planting and two new bridges on the Evans Creek Trail. The $19 million stormwater-funded project is in design; the city expects construction to start in 2027 and finish in 2028. City of Redmond

October 12, 2026

Avondale Road between NE 90th Street and Novelty Hill Road is next for about 2,500 feet of new water main, 3.2 lane miles of repaving and buffered bike lanes on both sides; the city expects construction to start this year and finish by late 2027. City of Redmond

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Redmond newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Redmond that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Redmond's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It goes to a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher only if you tick the sharing box on the form; that brokerage may then contact you about your home. Leave the box empty and it stays with us. Never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
